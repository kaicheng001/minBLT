The little byte workshop opens at 08:30. A lamp glows above a clean wooden table.
Ada writes a pattern: red blue red blue, 1 2 3, red blue red blue, 1 2 3.
Ben reads it aloud. A byte is a number; a patch is a group of bytes.
The group has no dictionary entry. Its meaning comes from the surrounding story.

Outside, rain taps the window: tap, tap, pause; tap, tap, pause.
Inside, the notebook says: Better than BPE? First test the idea carefully!
Predict the next byte from the past. Never borrow a letter from tomorrow.
Finish one patch, remember its shape, then begin the next small patch.

At noon they share a café snack. The price is €3, and the circle is marked π.
A snowman ☃ smiles on the page. These symbols occupy several UTF-8 bytes.
The bytes can cross a patch boundary. The original bytes still fit together.
Numbers repeat: 10, 20, 30; 10, 20, 30. Spaces and commas matter too.

Ada writes a second pattern: green gold green gold, 4 5 6, green gold green gold.
Ben checks every boundary. No byte is missing; no byte is counted twice.
The first prediction starts with empty context. Later predictions remember the past.
A short experiment teaches a small lesson. A careful test makes the lesson useful.

In the evening the lamp glows again. Rain taps, pencils move, and pages turn.
Red blue red blue, 1 2 3. Green gold green gold, 4 5 6.
Tomorrow brings another sequence. For now, save the model and close the notebook.
The little byte workshop closes at 18:30, ready for another careful experiment.
